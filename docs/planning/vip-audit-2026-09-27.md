# VIP audit and plan of action, 27 September 2026

A full-app review done three weeks after launch. It combines four sources: live production usage (Supabase), a screen-by-screen pass of the app at phone (390 px) and desktop (1440 px) widths, the code behind the core flows, and research into what competing apps do and what the reptile community praises or complains about. The launch review (`launch-review-2026-09-04.md`) and the mobile audit (`mobile-uiux-audit-2026-09-10.md`) covered security, performance and visual consistency. This one asks a different question: **why do people sign up and not come back, and what would make the core experience feel first class?**

## How to continue

1. Work on `main` (see CLAUDE.md).
2. Phase 0 shipped with this document. Start at Phase 1 below.
3. The three items under "Decisions needed" are Tennyson's calls. Nothing in Phase 1 that depends on them should ship until they are made.
4. When an item ships, change its status here and, if it maps to a ROADMAP.md item, check it off there too.

**Where people actually go (signed-in users, last 90 days):** My Geckos (22), Morph ID (18), My Profile (13), Membership (12), Dashboard (8), Lineage (8), Settings (5), Breeding (4). Everything else in the app had three or fewer signed-in users in 90 days. Forum, Gallery, Geck Answers, Community Connect, Marketplace Buy, Training, Giveaways and Morph Visualizer had zero or one.

**Where anonymous visitors go:** the landing page, the genetics calculator, the Morph Guide (Cappuccino, Axanthic, Lilly White pages lead), the blog, and the Care Guide. The reference content is the real front door.

**The takeaway in one line:** Geck Inspect has about 57 pages, but the product people touch is five screens: landing, My Geckos, Morph ID, the calculator and the Morph Guide. Those five deserve the VIP treatment first. Almost nothing downstream matters until a new keeper adds a gecko, gets something useful back, and has a reason to return.

## 2. What the community celebrates (research)

Competitor pages that were blocked from direct fetching were read through search summaries. Sources are listed at the end.

### What keepers praise

- **"It keeps me on schedule."** The most common praise in App Store reviews for crested gecko care apps (Exotic Reptile Care, Shed, CareTrack, Husbandry.Pro) is reminders that arrive on the phone, when the app is closed. Per-animal feeding schedules and push reminders are the norm.
- **One-tap logging.** SnekLog's "Quick Feed" logs a whole room in one click once a typical meal is set. HerpTracker markets three-tap logging. Speed of the daily log is the retention loop.
- **Weight charts that mean something.** Keepers search for "crested gecko weight chart by age". A plain line of grams is less useful than "is this normal for a 9-month-old?".
- **Low commitment.** Several of the praised apps work without an account or offline. Geckistry's AI morph identifier is free.

### What breeders praise

- **QR tub labels that open a quick log.** MorphMarket's Label Creator prints QR labels from the animal record. Scanning one asks "Edit the animal or log a husbandry activity?". This is exactly how breeders work at a rack.
- **Hatch countdowns and pip alerts.** HatchLedger and Cltch both lead with this.
- **Hatchlings that inherit genetics automatically.** Log the clutch, the babies arrive with parents, possible hets and lineage filled in.
- **One-click buyer packet.** HatchLedger's most marketed sales feature: lineage, genetics, feeding, weights and shed history in one document for the buyer.
- **Waitlists with deposits.** Breed Ledger, HatchLedger and the new Day56 all lead with this. HatchLedger publishes a case study of a waitlist generating $40K in pre-sales.
- **Shared access.** HerpTracker and ReptiDex both sell collaborators (a partner or staff member logs too), with an activity log so nobody double-feeds.

### What people complain about (across the category)

- Records lost or reminders that do not fire. Spreadsheets win when an app is unreliable.
- Paywalls on basics (for example "more than 3 animals").
- MorphMarket: scams, uneven buyer protection, slow support. Trust is an open lane.

### Crested-gecko-specific practice the app should reflect

- **Fired up and fired down.** The community's standard advice is to judge color and pattern from photos taken both fired up and fired down. The Morph ID flow already asks for fire state; the add-gecko and listing flows do not.
- **Breeding readiness.** The widely repeated rule: females at 40 g or more (many breeders want 45 g) and at least 12 to 18 months old before pairing. NEHERP and most care sheets say this. The landing page slideshow already promises "breeding readiness", but the app only has a manual "Ready to Breed" status.
- **CGD brands.** Pangea and Repashy are the reference diets. Feeding logs should speak that language.

### Competitor snapshot (September 2026)

| Product | What it leads with | Price signal |
|---|---|---|
| ReptiDex (Dusty) | iOS app, 137 species, pedigrees, weight charts, QR to live record | Free to 10 animals, $4.99 Pro, $9.99 Premium |
| Breed Ledger (Dusty) | Breeder website on your domain, waitlists with deposits, contracts, registry for clubs, genetics seeded from Geckistry | Subscription |
| Geckistry (Dusty) | Free AI morph identifier "trained on thousands of labeled crested gecko images" | Free |

| MorphMarket | Free husbandry app, label creator with QR quick log | Free with marketplace |
| SnekLog | "Most intuitive", one-click Quick Feed, feeding shopping list | Free |

### Where Geck Inspect already matches or leads

Crested-first genetics with the Cappuccino complex and possible hets, reverse calculator and Clutch Lab (nobody else has these), the evidence-first Morph ID, a real Morph Guide, lineage and pedigree with QR passports, Field Mode and Batch Husbandry for fast logging, hatch alerts and weekly weigh-in reminders on a server schedule, MorphMarket export, and a native shell in progress. The gaps are not feature count. They are access, reliability of the daily loop, and focus.

## 3. Priority areas, ranked by need

Ranked by (evidence of pain) x (how many users it touches) / (effort). Status values: **Shipped 27 Sep**, **Decision needed**, **Next**, **Later**.

### P1. First-run activation: signup to first gecko to first photo

- **Evidence:** 2 of 3 new accounts never add a gecko. 1 photo uploaded in 30 days.
- **What we found:** After signup the path is a role question, then a tour, then a Dashboard whose top half is community totals, then a card that links to My Geckos (not to the add form), then a 20+ field form where photos are the last field. The landing page promises "log a gecko in under a minute".
- **What to change:**
  1. Every "add your first gecko" button opens the add form directly. **Shipped 27 Sep** (`/MyGeckos?add=1`).
  2. A short "quick add" for a first gecko: photo, name, sex, hatch date (or "unknown"), optional morph. Everything else behind "More details". Photo first, not last. **Next**
  3. After the role question, skip the tour for keepers and go straight to quick add; offer the tour afterwards. **Next**
  4. After the first save, show one clear next step: "Log today's weight" or "Run Morph ID on this photo". **Next**
- **Done when:** at least 60% of new accounts add a gecko in their first session, and at least half of those include a photo.
- **Effort:** M.

### P2. Let people try Morph ID

- **Evidence:** 18 signed-in users opened Morph ID in 90 days; 6 identifications have ever run. Free accounts have 0 credits, so the flagship feature ends in an upgrade card for every free user. Geckistry's identifier is free. The July pricing decision (`docs/specs/pricing-decision-2026-07.md`) listed 1 free ID per month; the code later set it to 0.
- **What to change:** give free accounts a small taste. Options are in "Decisions needed". Whichever is chosen, the result screen should end with "Save as a new gecko", which already exists and pre-fills the add form.
- **Done when:** Morph ID runs per week is above the number of new signups per week.
- **Effort:** S once decided (one number in `src/lib/tierLimits.js`, mirrored in `consume_morph_id_credit()` and the `recognize-gecko-morph` function).
- **Status:** Decision needed.

### P3. Reminders that work when the app is closed

- **Evidence:** feeding reminders run in `src/components/feeding/FeedingAlertSystem.jsx`, a 5-minute loop inside an open browser tab. Zero feeding-due notifications went out in the last 30 days. Hatch alerts and weigh-in reminders already run on a server schedule (pg_cron) and deliver by email and push through `notify_dispatch_on_insert`, so the pipe exists.
- **What to change:** a daily `enqueue_feeding_reminders()` cron job modeled on `enqueue_weighin_reminders()`. One batched message per keeper per day ("3 geckos are due for CGD today"), never one per gecko, and only for keepers who set a feeding schedule. Keep the in-tab banner as the in-app surface.
- **Done when:** a keeper with a feeding group gets one reminder on the due day with the app closed, and none on other days.
- **Effort:** S to M (migration, function, cron entry, a Settings toggle).
- **Status:** Decision needed (it starts sending email and push to real people).

### P4. Dashboard: "your geckos today" first, community second

- **Evidence:** only 8 signed-in users opened the Dashboard in 90 days, while 22 opened My Geckos. The Dashboard's first screen on a phone is a large greeting and community totals (keepers, geckos, forum threads). The useful part, Next Actions (eggs near hatching, geckos not weighed in 30 days, pairings that went quiet), sits below the fold.
- **What to change:**
  1. Next Actions moves above the stats strip for signed-in keepers; the hero is smaller on phones. **Shipped 27 Sep**
  2. Guests were greeted as "Geck" (the fallback name "Geck Inspect User" cut to its first word). Guests and nameless accounts now get the greeting without a name. **Shipped 27 Sep**
  3. Next: a "Today" card that merges feedings due, weigh-ins due and eggs near hatch, each with a one-tap log button, and hide community totals while the numbers are small (showing "5 forum threads" advertises an empty room). **Next**
- **Effort:** S (shipped part), M (Today card).

### P5. The gecko record: crested-first depth

These are the features that make a crested gecko keeper feel understood, and that multi-species competitors cannot copy cheaply.

1. **Breeding readiness badge on females.** Computed from the latest weight and age: "Ready by weight and age" (40 g or more and 12 months or more), "Close" or "Not yet", with the reason. Shown on the card, the detail page and in the pairing planner, where it warns before an underweight female is paired. The landing page already promises this. **Next** (S to M)
2. **Healthy growth band on the weight chart.** Shade the typical crested gecko range by age behind the user's line so "is 18 g normal at 8 months?" answers itself. Source the band from the care guide's own numbers so the app does not contradict itself. **Next** (S)
3. **Fired-up and fired-down photo slots.** Two labeled photo slots on the add and listing forms, reusing the fire-state logic Morph ID already has. Buyers ask for both; the listing shows both. **Later** (M)
4. **Owner quick log from the QR passport.** When the owner scans a tub label, the passport page shows "Quick log: fed, weight, shed" at the top, opening Field Mode with that gecko selected. Today the owner sees buyer-facing content and two buttons at the bottom. **Next** (S)

### P6. Guest demo experience

- **Evidence:** 31 guest-mode sessions and 49 landing CTA clicks in 30 days. The guest notice was a 160 px yellow card floating over the middle of every phone screen, covering gecko cards, and its "Create account" button opened the Sign In tab.
- **What changed:** on phones the notice is now one slim 54 px bar ("Guest demo, sample data only", a Sign up button, an expand control and close). Every sign-up button in it opens the Create Account tab instead of Sign In. Desktop is unchanged. **Shipped 27 Sep**
- **Next:** a guided three-stop demo (My Geckos, one gecko's record with weights and lineage, the calculator) with a "save this as my own" moment at the end. **Later** (M)

### P7. Focus: fewer, better surfaces

- **Evidence:** about 57 routed pages; eight community and discovery pages had zero or one signed-in visitor in 90 days. There are 4 forum posts and 10 Q&A questions in total. Empty community rooms make the whole product feel empty, and each page is something that can break.
- **What to change:** hide pages from navigation (not delete) until there is density: Giveaways, Mentorship, Community Connect, Geck Answers (fold its questions into the Forum), Training and Train Model (admin only), the duplicate Genetic Calculator Tool (point it at `/calculator`), Supplies. Keep Forum but move it lower. The sidebar grouping in `src/lib/navItems.js` makes this a one-file change.
- **Status:** Decision needed (see below). **Effort:** S.

### P8. Messaging and trust fixes

1. The landing page carried a "Crested-only today. Gargoyle & Leachianus next." callout promising a free species upgrade. That contradicts DECISIONS.md entry 1 and INTENT.md ("no coming soon to more reptiles language"). Removed. **Shipped 27 Sep**
2. The landing hero pill says "Welcome To The Geck OS". "Geck OS" is not used anywhere else and does not tell a first-time visitor anything. Replace with a crested-specific proof point (for example "Built only for crested geckos"). **Next** (XS)
3. Morph ID page heading read "Crested gecko Morph ID" (mixed case) and the page had no title of its own in the browser tab. Fixed. **Shipped 27 Sep**
4. The Membership page has no page heading (flagged in the mobile audit). Added. **Shipped 27 Sep**
5. The pricing page now says records export to CSV or PDF on every plan (ROADMAP item 18). Lock-in fear is a top complaint across the category. Still to do: the same line on the landing page. **Shipped 27 Sep** (pricing page)

### P9. Breeder revenue features (after P1 to P5)

Worth building once there are enough active breeders to test with. Each one maps to something competitors are winning on.

1. **Buyer packet** from the passport: one PDF with photos (fired up and down), lineage, genetics and possible hets, weight history, feeding notes, and the passport QR. Most of the pieces exist (`certificateExport.js`, `AnimalPassport`). **Later** (M)
2. **Waitlist with deposits** tied to a pairing's projected outcomes (the calculator already knows the odds). **Later** (L)
3. **Collaborators with an activity log** on a collection (collections and collection members already exist in the schema). **Later** (M)

### P10. Design debt (carried from the mobile audit)

The P1 to P8 feature pages (Animal Passport, Batch Husbandry, Geck Answers, Breeding ROI, Breeding Loans, Market Pricing, Claim Animal, Printable Worksheets) still use a separate inline palette and fonts. The Animal Passport is the one that matters most, because buyers see it. Convert it first; leave the low-traffic ones until P7 decides whether they stay. Touch targets: the most-used button size is 32 px; raise it on the five core screens. **Later** (M)

## 4. Plan of action

### Phase 0, shipped with this document (27 September)

- Guest greeting no longer reads "Geck".
- Dashboard: Next Actions sits above the stats for signed-in keepers; hero is smaller on phones.
- Every "add your first gecko" action opens the add form directly (`/MyGeckos?add=1`).
- Guest notice is a slim bar on phones and its sign-up buttons open the Create Account tab.
- My Geckos: Add Gecko leads the header on phones (full width, above the secondary tools).
- Pricing page states that records export to CSV or PDF on every plan (ROADMAP item 18 messaging).
- Landing multi-species callout removed.
- Morph ID heading and browser title fixed; Membership page heading added.

### Phase 1, activation and the daily loop (next two weeks)

1. Quick add for the first gecko, photo first (P1.2).
2. Keeper onboarding goes to quick add, tour after (P1.3).
3. First-save next step (P1.4).
4. Free Morph ID taste, once decided (P2).
5. Server-side feeding reminders, once decided (P3).
6. "Today" card on the Dashboard (P4.3).
7. Hero pill and the export trust line on the landing page (P8.2, P8.5).

**Measure:** share of new accounts that add a gecko in session one; share that return on day 2 and day 7; Morph ID runs per week. The PostHog events for these already exist (`first_gecko_added`, `login_completed`, `onboarding_role_selected`).

### Phase 2, crested-first depth (weeks 3 to 6)

1. Breeding readiness badge and pairing warning (P5.1).
2. Growth band on the weight chart (P5.2).
3. QR passport quick log for owners (P5.4).
4. Navigation focus pass (P7), once decided.
5. Animal Passport onto the main design system (P10).

### Phase 3, breeder revenue (weeks 6 to 12)

1. Buyer packet (P9.1).
2. Fired-up and fired-down photo slots (P5.3).
3. Collaborators (P9.3).
4. Waitlist with deposits (P9.2).

### Not now

Structure AI grading, IoT expansion, international pricing and a registry are all still good ideas in STRATEGY.md. None of them fixes the fact that 19 of 24 new accounts do not come back on a second day. Revisit after Phase 1 moves that number.

---

## 5. Decisions needed

1. **Free Morph ID.** Options: (a) one free identification per account, ever; (b) one per month, as the July pricing doc proposed; (c) keep 0. Recommendation: (a). It costs a few cents per new account, answers Geckistry's free identifier, and ends the dead end 18 users hit.
2. **Server-side feeding reminders.** Recommendation: on for keepers who have set a feeding schedule, one batched message per day, with an off switch in Settings and in every message.
3. **Hide low-traffic pages from navigation.** Recommendation: hide the eight listed in P7 from the sidebar and command palette; keep their URLs working so nothing breaks for the few people who bookmarked them.
