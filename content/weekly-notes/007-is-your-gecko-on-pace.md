Crested geckos have four life stages: hatchlings under 3 g (to 3 months),
juveniles 3 to 15 g (to 12 months), sub-adults 15 to 35 g (to 18 months) and
adults from 35 g. A stage ends at its weight or its age, whichever is later. A
10 g gecko at 14 months is still a juvenile.

A typical gecko weighs about 7 g at 6 months, 15 g at 12 and 35 g at 18. Real
geckos run ahead or behind, so judge the trend over several weigh-ins.

The weight chart appears after the first weigh-in. With a hatch date, it
shades a typical range for its age. The range is wide, so also check the gain:
a juvenile usually puts on 1 to 4 g a month.

This week, open a gecko, add its hatch date if you know it, and log a weight
if it has none. If the line stays below that range, or a juvenile gains under
1 g a month for two weigh-ins in a row, keep weighing weekly and check feeding
and temperatures.

No gecko yet? Add one with a first weight, and its hatch date if you know it.
