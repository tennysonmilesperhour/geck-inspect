Sale listings often put a label next to the morph: 100% het or 66% possible
het. These labels are for recessive genes such as Axanthic and Phantom. Each
one is a claim about the parents, not about how the gecko looks.

100% het means the parentage guarantees it. A baby from a visual Axanthic and
a non-carrier carries one copy of Axanthic.

66% possible het is a normal-looking baby from two hets. On paper, two in
three of those babies carry the gene, and there is no way to tell which ones.

Het status cannot be read from looks. Only the parentage, or a test pairing,
backs it up. A pos het label with no parents named is often an educated guess,
and you cannot check it. Proven means documented, controlled breeding results,
not appearance.

Open your family trees and pick a gecko with a blank parent. Tap the Unknown
Sire or Unknown Dam card and fill it in from the breeder's listing, with their
het label in the notes. No gecko yet? The Genetics Guide explains each of
these labels.
