Axanthic is recessive. A crested gecko needs two copies of the gene to show
it. The gene removes yellow and red, so a visual Axanthic looks black, gray
and white. A het carries one copy and looks normal.

Pair two hets of the same Axanthic line and each egg has the same odds: 25%
visual Axanthic, 50% het, and 25% non-carrier (no copy at all).

That is where "66% possible het" comes from. Out of every four babies, on
average, one is visual and three look normal. Two of those three carry the
gene, and two in three is the 66% on sale listings. Nobody can tell which two
by looking, because a het looks just like a non-carrier. Only test breeding
can show it.

Pair a visual Axanthic with a non-carrier and every baby is a 100% het.

These odds are per egg, not a promise per clutch. Four eggs do not guarantee
one visual. A gray photo is not proof either. Lighting, photo editing and a
gecko firing up or down can make a normal crested gecko look gray.

Run the het x het pairing in the calculator to see the split.
