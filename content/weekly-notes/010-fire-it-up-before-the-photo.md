The same crested gecko can look like two different animals. Most crested
geckos fire up at night or right after a misting: darker and more saturated.
They fire down by day, at rest or when stressed, and look muted. Both are
normal. It is a temporary color change, not genetics.

That matters for photos. Fired-down photos make animals hard to compare across
a collection and can mislead a buyer or a breeder.

Before a record photo or a Morph ID, mist the enclosure and leave the gecko in
it until its color deepens. Then take a top photo and a side photo. For a
Lavender or a possible Sable, add a fired-down third photo. Lavender shows
most clearly fired down, and a Sable stays dark even then.

Morph ID suggests what a photo can show: how the gecko looks. Het status and
proven genetics need the parents, the pedigree or test breeding. When the
result fits, tap Add to my collection to pre-fill the photos and suggested
labels. Check each field before you save.

fired-up gecko.
