Lilly White is incomplete dominant, so one copy is enough for the trait to
show. Two copies are lethal, and that is what shapes how breeders pair it.

Pair a Lilly White to a gecko without Lilly White and about half the eggs are
Lilly Whites. None of them is a Super Lilly White.

Pair two Lilly Whites and the eggs split about a quarter Super Lilly White,
half Lilly White and a quarter normal. Super Lilly White embryos die in the
egg or within days of hatching. So the second pairing gives the same share of
Lilly Whites and loses about a quarter of the eggs.

The same goes for any two geckos that both carry Lilly White, Frappuccinos
included. White coverage alone does not prove a gecko is a Lilly White. Check
the lineage of both animals, along with their tail, flank and belly markings,
before you set up a pairing.

Open the Lilly White x normal pairing in the calculator and see the odds for
each egg.
