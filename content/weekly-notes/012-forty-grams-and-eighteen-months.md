A female crested gecko needs two numbers before her first season: at least 40
g and at least 18 months old. Both, not either. A heavy female who is only a
year old is not ready yet, and neither is an older female who is still light.

Those are minimums. Most experienced breeders wait longer, for 45 to 55 g at
24 months or more. A male can start sooner, at about 25 to 30 g and 12 months.

The female is the limiting factor. Breeding a female too early is the most
common way keepers lose one, through calcium loss or egg binding.

Geck Inspect checks both numbers for you. The Breeding readiness card on each
female's record says what is missing, such as more weight or more age. It
needs a weight and a hatch date before it can say she is ready.

If you keep females, open each one in your collection this week. Add her hatch
date if it is missing, and log a current weight. Then read what her readiness
card says before you plan a pairing.
