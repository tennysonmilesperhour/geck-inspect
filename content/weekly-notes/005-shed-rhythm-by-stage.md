Growth drives shedding, so how often a crested gecko sheds depends on its life
stage. Most hatchlings shed every 1 to 2 weeks. Most juveniles shed every 2 to
3 weeks. Most sub-adults shed every 3 to 4 weeks. Most adults shed every 4 to
6 weeks. These are typical ranges, not rules. A fast-growing gecko sheds at
the short end.

You often will not see it. A shed takes about 10 to 20 minutes, usually
overnight, and the gecko eats its own skin. The tell is a dull, chalky look
one night and bright color the next morning. Afterwards, check the toes and
the tail tip. Stuck skin there almost always means the humidity is too low.

Log the last shed in Field Mode. You can pick the day, so a shed from last
week still counts. That starts the next shed window on the record. After three
logged sheds, the window follows your gecko's own rhythm instead of the
typical cycle for its stage.
