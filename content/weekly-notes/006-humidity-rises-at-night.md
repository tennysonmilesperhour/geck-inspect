Crested geckos do not need one humidity number. They need a daily cycle: high
at night, when they are active, and lower by day.

Aim for 80 to 100% at night. By day, let it fall back to 50 to 70% within a
few hours.

Mist heavily about 30 minutes before lights-out, so the enclosure is visibly
wet. Then let it dry. In the morning, mist lightly or not at all if the
enclosure is still damp. A shallow water dish also helps buffer humidity.

Both extremes cause trouble. An enclosure that stays wet all the time breeds
mold and respiratory infections. One that stays dry all the time causes stuck
shed.

Stuck shed on the toes or the tail tip almost always means humidity is running
too low. Deal with stuck toe shed within 48 hours, or the toe can be lost.
Soak the gecko on damp paper towel for about 15 minutes, then ease the shed
off gently with a damp cotton swab. Never pull. If it does not come off
easily, soak longer.

Open the humidity guide and check your evening routine against it.
