An adult female crested gecko can lay eggs even with no male. Those eggs are
infertile, but she still needs a safe place to lay them.

A lay box is a deli cup or plastic tub with a hole cut in the lid. Fill it
with 3 to 4 inches of coco fiber or sphagnum that is moist but not wet. Keep
it in the enclosure all the time.

Without one, she may lay in the substrate or hold the eggs. If she has a firm,
swollen belly, strains without laying and turns lethargic, she may be egg
bound. That needs an emergency vet.

Collect eggs within 24 hours. If she has been with a male, mark the top of
each egg with a pencil dot and never turn it, since turning can kill the
embryo. After laying she needs to rebuild calcium, so keep offering a
high-calcium crested gecko diet.

If she has been paired, add the clutch to her breeding plan in Breeding with
the day it was laid. The expected hatch date follows your incubation
temperature setting.
