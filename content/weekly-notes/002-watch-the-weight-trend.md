In a crested gecko, a falling weight is usually the first sign of a problem.
It tends to show before appetite changes and before any loss is visible.

Trends only work when the numbers are comparable. Weigh on the same scale each
time, in a deli cup with a lid, and before a feeding, so the last meal does
not inflate the reading.

A healthy adult holds within about 2 g from month to month. Breeding females
swing more around a lay, so expect bigger moves from them then.

Look into any drop of 5% or more over a month. If the weight keeps falling
across two months, book a vet visit.

This week, open a gecko's page and read its weight chart as a line, not as a
last number. If none of your geckos are on record yet, add one and log its
first weight, so the line has a start.
