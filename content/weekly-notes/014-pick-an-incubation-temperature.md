If you have eggs on the way, pick one incubation temperature and hold it
steady. The temperature you pick sets the hatch window.

Days count from the lay date. At 72 to 75°F, the hobby default, eggs usually
hatch in 70 to 90 days. At 68 to 71°F, eggs take 90 to 120 days. At 76 to
78°F, eggs take 60 to 75 days.

Keep incubation below about 80°F. Breeders report more deformed and weak
hatchlings from hot incubation. Some breeders report that temperature shifts
the sex, but that has not been shown in crested geckos, so do not count on it.

Too wet is the most common egg killer, so keep standing water out of the
incubation medium. Check the eggs once a week. If the medium dries, mist the
container walls, never the eggs.

Geck Inspect uses the same three ranges for hatch estimates and hatch alerts.
Open Breeding, pick your range on the Hatchery tab, then add each clutch with
its lay date from its breeding plan.
